# Press-Print Hierarchy Stabilization
Version: 2.0
Status: System Extension
Scope: General handling of diffuse, repetitive, sparse, or ambiguous source hierarchy

## 0. Purpose

This document closes a gap between Press-Print's existing visual grammar and runtime behavior.

The existing research already requires stronger hierarchy than the source, functional low-information fields, source-derived direction, and cause-level critique. The missing step was an explicit rule for what to do when the source itself does not provide a sufficiently useful hierarchy.

Hierarchy stabilization is **not** a new user-facing mode and does not replace the seven canonical research outputs. It operationalizes concepts already present in the core constitution, visual grammar, art-direction policy, schema, and benchmark.

## 1. Core rule

> **Do not inherit weak source hierarchy by default. Resolve structural ambiguity before stylization.**

A source may need hierarchy stabilization when attention is spread too evenly, many similar subjects compete at the same level, the composition is dominated by large low-information fields, or no anchor is strong enough to carry the intended reconstruction.

Do not use rigid percentage, coverage, or object-count thresholds as universal triggers. Evaluate whether the source hierarchy can support a decisive Press-Print reconstruction.

## 2. Repetition resolution

When many similar elements compete at comparable visual weight:

1. Identify whether a single element, a small cluster, or the repeated group itself is the best dominant unit.
2. Promote one **dominant anchor or dominant cluster** when that improves the reading.
3. Retain a small number of supporting anchors or clusters when useful.
4. Reinterpret the remaining repetition as rhythm, field, mass, texture, or context.
5. Preserve group identity when repetition itself is semantically or structurally important.

Useful differentiation operations include:

- crop,
- isolation,
- scale contrast,
- selective photographic retention,
- detail reduction,
- contrast reduction,
- grouping,
- flattening,
- suppression,
- controlled halftone or graphic treatment.

The goal is not to erase repetition. The goal is to stop repetition from flattening hierarchy.

> **Repetition should be organized as hierarchy, not preserved as visual equality.**

## 3. Low-information-field resolution

A substantial quiet region must be interpreted as a compositional resource rather than as missing content.

Possible roles include:

- separation,
- pause,
- directional room,
- scale buffer,
- atmospheric release,
- semantic isolation,
- continuation,
- framing,
- graphic mass.

Once a role is assigned, reconstruction should reinforce that role through source-derived color, planar treatment, restrained materiality, framing relationships, edge pressure, or continued quietness.

Do not fill quiet space merely because it is quiet. Do not introduce unrelated scenery, objects, symbols, or decoration simply to increase visual activity.

If a low-information region is genuinely redundant rather than functional, crop, compress, suppress, or reduce it.

## 4. Anchor selection

A dominant anchor or cluster may be selected using:

- semantic importance,
- useful visual salience,
- positional leverage,
- color anchoring,
- relational importance,
- capacity to support the direction thesis,
- compatibility with source identity.

Semantic anchor and visual anchor remain distinct. Hierarchy stabilization may align them, separate them, or rebalance them.

## 5. Direction integration

When hierarchy stabilization is needed, the direction hypothesis should answer:

- What becomes dominant?
- What supports it?
- What becomes field / rhythm / context?
- What role do major quiet regions perform?

The final result should establish a readable dominant / secondary / quiet organization without manufacturing hierarchy at the cost of source identity.

## 6. Critique integration

A hierarchy-stabilized output should fail critique when:

- repeated similar elements remain equally salient despite the need for differentiation,
- a single-object hero is forced where the group relation is identity-bearing,
- a major quiet field has no compositional function,
- quiet space is arbitrarily filled with unrelated content,
- hierarchy is created mainly through decorative effects rather than structural operations,
- the source becomes less specific or less recognizable as a result of stabilization.

When revision is needed, change the hierarchy cause first: anchor selection, grouping, crop, scale contrast, suppression, or field role. Do not hide weak hierarchy under more material texture.

## 7. Relationship to Press-Print identity

Hierarchy stabilization exists to strengthen the existing Press-Print identity:

- v1 visual force remains the rendering authority,
- v2 source intelligence decides where that force should land,
- low-information fields remain active rather than passive,
- repeated fields retain source rhythm without becoming visually democratic,
- materiality remains downstream of structure.

The desired result is still:

> **v1.0.2 visual force + v2.0 source intelligence.**
